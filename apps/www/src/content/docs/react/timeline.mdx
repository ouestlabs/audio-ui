## Anatomy

Import the primitive and assemble its parts.

```tsx
import { Timeline } from "@audio-ui/react";

<Timeline.Root>
  <Timeline.Viewport>
    <Timeline.Ruler>
      <Timeline.Mark value={0} />
      <Timeline.Playhead />
    </Timeline.Ruler>
    <Timeline.Track>
      <Timeline.TrackHeader>
        <Timeline.TrackResizeHandle />
      </Timeline.TrackHeader>
      <Timeline.Lane />
    </Timeline.Track>
    <Timeline.Lane>
      <Timeline.Range>
        <Timeline.RangeHandle edge="start" />
        <Timeline.RangeHandle edge="end" />
      </Timeline.Range>
      <Timeline.Clip>
        <Timeline.ClipHeader />
        <Timeline.ClipFade edge="in" />
        <Timeline.ClipFade edge="out" />
        <Timeline.ClipHandle edge="start" />
        <Timeline.ClipHandle edge="end" />
        <Timeline.ClipFadeHandle edge="in" />
        <Timeline.ClipFadeHandle edge="out" />
      </Timeline.Clip>
      <Timeline.Playhead />
    </Timeline.Lane>
  </Timeline.Viewport>
  <Timeline.Navigator>
    <Timeline.NavigatorWindow />
    <Timeline.NavigatorHandle edge="start" />
    <Timeline.NavigatorHandle edge="end" />
  </Timeline.Navigator>
</Timeline.Root>
```

## API reference

### Root

Groups the parts of a timeline and owns what they share: its length, its zoom, its grid and the playback position. Units are yours: seconds, beats, pulses or steps. `zoom` is how many px one unit takes; leave it unset and the timeline fits the width it is given. The position comes from `position`, or from a live `source` that the playhead follows straight in the DOM without re-rendering React. With a `snapStep`, every gesture lands on the grid unless Cmd/Ctrl is held, and `snapPoints` (the edges of other clips, the locators) pull a dragged clip or range onto them from a few px away. Renders a `<div>` element.

<TypeTable>
<TypeRow name={"defaultPosition"} type={"number"} default={"0"}>Uncontrolled starting position.</TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"defaultZoom"} type={"number"}>Uncontrolled starting zoom, in px per unit. Unset, the timeline fits its width until zoomed.</TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"disabled"} type={"boolean"}></TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"duration"} type={"number"} default={"1"}>Length of the timeline, in your units.</TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"largeStep"} type={"number"}>Step for Page Up/Page Down and Shift + arrow keys. Defaults to ten `step`s.</TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"maxZoom"} type={"number"} default={"Infinity"}></TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"minZoom"} type={"number"} default={"0"}></TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"onPositionChange"} type={"(value: number, eventDetails: PositionChangeEventDetails) => void"}>Fires while the position is moved: press, drag, keyboard and input changes.</TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"onPositionCommitted"} type={"(value: number, eventDetails: PositionCommitEventDetails) => void"}>Fires when a gesture that moved the position ends.</TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"onZoomChange"} type={"(zoom: number, eventDetails: ZoomChangeEventDetails) => void"}>Fires when the zoom changes, already clamped. `eventDetails.cancel()` keeps the zoom where it was.</TypeRow>

<TypeRow name={"snapOffset"} type={"number"} default={"0"}>Where the grid's lines start, in your units.</TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"snapPoints"} type={"readonly number[]"}>Places that pull a press, or a dragged clip or range, onto them from a few px away, ahead of the grid: the edges of other clips, the locators. Cmd/Ctrl lets go of them too.</TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"snapStep"} type={"number"} default={"0"}>Distance between grid lines, in your units. 0 is no grid.</TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"source"} type={"Source"}>A live position followed outside React; takes precedence over `position`.</TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"step"} type={"number"}>Step for the arrow keys. Defaults to `snapStep`, or a hundredth of `duration`.</TypeRow>

### Navigator

The whole timeline as one bar, with the stretch the viewport shows drawn inside it as `Timeline.NavigatorWindow`: drag the window to scroll, drag one of its `Timeline.NavigatorHandle`s to zoom, double-click to show everything. Place it anywhere inside `Timeline.Root`, outside the viewport. Renders a `<div>` element.

<TypeTable>
<TypeRow name={"getAriaValueText"} type={"DescribePosition"}>Returns how a position is announced by the window and its handles. Defaults to the number.</TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"minWindowWidth"} type={"number"}>The least width the window is drawn at, in px, so its handles keep room at any zoom. Dragged, the window follows the pointer, and the view follows the window. Leave it out and the window is as wide as the stretch it shows.</TypeRow>
</TypeTable>

<TypeTable title="Data attributes">

### NavigatorWindow

The stretch of the timeline the viewport shows. Dragging it scrolls; its native `<input type="range">` scrolls from the keyboard and carries its accessible name. Renders a `<div>` element.

### Viewport

The scrolling window onto the timeline. Cmd/Ctrl + wheel (and pinch on a trackpad) zooms around the pointer; a bare wheel scrolls as usual. Every zoom change keeps its anchor point in place, or the middle of the view when there is none. A clip moved or trimmed with the pointer held near its edges scrolls it. It reports its scroll offset and width for `Timeline.Navigator`. Renders a `<div>` element.

<TypeTable title="Data attributes">
<TypeRow name={"data-disabled"}>Present when disabled.</TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"data-fitted"}>Whether no zoom is set, so the timeline fits the width it is given.</TypeRow>
<TypeRow name={"data-scrubbing"}>Whether a pointer is moving the position.</TypeRow>
</TypeTable>

### Lane

A strip along the timeline that its parts are placed on: a track, the ruler's strip, a row of markers. Zoomed, it is as long as the timeline; fitted, it takes the width it is given and the timeline is spread across it. Its `--timeline-unit` CSS variable is the width of one unit. With `seekable`, a press on its bare area moves the position, as on the ruler, so the cursor can be put anywhere along a track; with `onSelectionChange` too, a drag from there selects a stretch of time instead of scrubbing. Renders a `<div>` element.

The drag then no longer scrubs. `eventDetails.cancel()` keeps the selection as it was.</TypeRow>

### Track

One track of the arrangement: a row holding a header beside a lane. Renders a `<div>` element.

<TypeRow name={"minHeight / maxHeight"} type={"number"} default={"32 / 480"}>Bounds the height is clamped to.</TypeRow>

### TrackHeader

The strip beside a track's lane that names it and holds what sets it. It stays at the left edge while the lane scrolls under it, at the stacking level of the `--timeline-track-header-z` CSS variable (20): raise it while the header is hovered or focused if it shows something that reaches into the next row, such as a value readout. It carries the track's `data-selected` and `data-resizing`. Renders a `<div>` element.

### TrackResizeHandle

Sets the track's height: by dragging, or from the keyboard through the native `<input type="range">` it holds, which carries the accessible name. The arrows move by a step (Down is taller), Page Up and Page Down by a large one, Home and End to the minimum and the maximum. Place it along the track's lower edge. A double-click clears the height, so the track shares the room again. Renders a `<div>` element.

### Ruler

It holds the native `<input type="range">` that takes focus, handles the keyboard and carries the accessible name (`aria-label` / `aria-labelledby`). Place `Timeline.Mark`s in it for its labels. Renders a `<div>` element.

### Range

A stretch of the timeline, such as a loop, a selection or a clip, laid on the lane it sits in from `value[0]` to `value[1]`. Its own native `<input type="range">` moves it from the keyboard and carries its accessible name (`aria-label` / `aria-labelledby`): Left and Right by a step, Up and Down by its own length, and with Cmd/Ctrl, Left and Right shorten or lengthen it by a step while Up and Down double or halve it. A drag lands an edge on a `snapPoints` place within a few px. Renders a `<div>` element.

### Clip

A stretch of a source laid on a lane. Dragging its `Timeline.ClipHeader` moves it, on the grid unless Cmd/Ctrl is held, and over other lanes too, or to the nearest when the pointer is off them; held near an edge of the view, a move or a trim scrolls it, and a move scrolls down to the lanes below too; pressing its body selects it and puts the cursor there. Add a `Timeline.ClipHandle` per edge to trim it and a `Timeline.ClipFadeHandle` per fade. It sets `--clip-offset`, `--clip-source-length`, `--clip-fade-in` and `--clip-fade-out` for its content, and `--clip-fade-in-max` and `--clip-fade-out-max`, how long each fade can grow, to draw the limit of a fade being dragged (`data-fading`), and `--clip-unit`, the width of one unit for what is placed inside it. Its own native `<input type="range">` moves it from the keyboard and carries its accessible name (`aria-label` / `aria-labelledby`); Delete and Backspace on it call `onRemove`. Renders a `<div>` element.
