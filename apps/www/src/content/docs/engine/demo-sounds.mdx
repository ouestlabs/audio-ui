A device preview plays a loop through the effect so you can hear what it does. Those loops, like the noise behind the equalizer and the pulses behind the compressor, are not recordings: each is drawn in code with oscillators, filters and noise, once per sample rate, then looped as a buffer. They all live in one file, `demo-sound`, and each has a name.

## Listen

One at a time, through the same master bus as a preview.

## Ask for one by name

```bash
npx shadcn@latest add @audio-ui/demo-sound
```

`DEMO_SOURCES` holds every sound by name, with a title and what to listen for. `renderDemoSource` draws one for a sample rate and keeps it, so the same buffer comes back each time.

```tsx
import { renderDemoSource } from "@/lib/demo-sound";

const buffer = await renderDemoSource("groove", ctx.sampleRate);
const source = ctx.createBufferSource();
source.buffer = buffer;
source.loop = true;
source.connect(output);
source.start();
```

A device chooses its own sound. `useDeviceLoop` plays one through an engine, gives back its tempo for a time parameter that syncs to it, and a session lets a preview's bar change the sound:

```tsx
import { useDemoSource } from "@/hooks/use-demo-source";
import { useDeviceLoop } from "@/hooks/use-device-loop";

// A de-esser plays the voice by default; a gate plays a held chord and is keyed by the kick that goes with it.
useDeviceLoop({ create, destination, source: "voice", state });
```

`useDemoSource("voice")` returns the sound chosen in the nearest `AudioSessionProvider`, or `"voice"` when nobody has chosen. Outside a session it is always the default.

## How they are made

A **drum hit** is a few nodes. The kick is a sine oscillator whose pitch falls fast, from 150 Hz to 40 Hz in 150 milliseconds, through a gain that decays: the body of a drum is a pitch that drops. The snare is noise through a band-pass, with a short tone under it. The hihat is noise through a high-pass at 7 kHz that dies in about fifty milliseconds. The [Synthesis](/docs/engine/synthesis#drum-hits) page has the voices.

The **groove** is two bars at 90 BPM, and most of what makes it feel played is small. The off-beat sixteenths are pushed back by a fraction of a step, which is the swing. The snare has quiet ghost notes in front of its backbeat, the hats are harder on the beat than between, a hat or two is left open, and each hit lands a few milliseconds early or late and a little harder or softer, from a fixed sequence so it is the same every time it is drawn. Under them a sine bass with a triangle an octave up plays the root of each chord, so it is heard on a small speaker too. An electric piano is four sine partials per note, a twin a few cents sharp on the other side of the stereo field whose beating moves the sound, and a lowpass over them, struck on an A minor seventh and then an F major seventh, with the notes of each chord a few milliseconds apart. What rings past the end of the loop is folded back into its start, so no note is cut where it turns.

The **hip-hop** scene is dark trap in half-time at 140 BPM. It is straight, since trap does not swing, and it is sparse: the room between the sounds is part of it. There is one clap on the third beat of each bar, four short bursts of band-passed noise a hair apart with the last one longer, and a snare that climbs into the end of the loop so the turn has a lift. The kick is short and there are five in two bars, each with an 808 note on it, to leave the weight room. The hats run on straight eighths, the ones on the beat harder than the ones between, with a soft sixteenth now and then just ahead of a beat, and two rolls placed away from the clap and the kick: triplets at the end of the first bar and thirty-second notes early in the second, each hit a little harder and higher in pitch than the last. The 808 is a sine that slides from one pitch to the next in a tenth of a second, and it goes two ways: a clean sub, low-passed, so the weight is never in the way, and the same note driven hard through a clip and high-passed, which is the bite. Over it a piano plays a short line in F minor. Each note is a few sine partials, each a touch sharp of its harmonic as a string is, the higher ones dying faster, with a soft click of felt at the strike, through a low-pass that takes the brightness off. The notes of the line sit a semitone apart, E against F, B flat against C, which is what makes it uneasy, and the first note of each bar comes again an octave down. A few high bells ring far off, and a pad of detuned saws through a low-pass swells in under each bar. It has its own tempo, so a delay synced to it echoes on its beat, and its own kick for a gate to listen to.

A **pluck** is a triangle oscillator through a low-pass filter that closes as the note dies, from 4 kHz down to 500 Hz, with a gain that falls to silence. The filter makes it sound like something struck rather than switched. It is the line of notes on top of the groove, there to give a delay or a reverb something to carry.

```ts
oscillator.type = "triangle";
filter.type = "lowpass";
filter.frequency.setValueAtTime(4000, time);
filter.frequency.exponentialRampToValueAtTime(500, time + 0.45);
gain.gain.setValueAtTime(0.28, time);
gain.gain.exponentialRampToValueAtTime(0.001, time + 0.45);
```

The **held chord** is four oscillators on an A minor chord that never stop. A signal that is never quiet by itself is what a gate has to open and close, and what a compressor ducks when the kick keys it.

The **voice** is a sawtooth at 140 Hz through two band-pass filters at 700 Hz and 1.2 kHz, the formants of a vowel, with a burst of noise through a high-pass at 5.5 kHz on four beats: the "s" a de-esser takes off.

The **wide chords** are the groove in the centre with a pad on each side. Each ear has its own oscillators, a little detuned from the other, and a low note that differs between them, so there is bass in the sides: a stereo image for a mid/side device to narrow or widen.

**Pink noise** has equal energy in each octave, which is why a flat equalizer looks flat on a spectrum and each band you move shows. It is white noise through a filter. The **pulses** are noise bursts that start loud and fade, eight to a loop, every other one quieter, so a level that follows them moves along a compressor's transfer curve.
