Thickens the sound with a short delay that a slow oscillator sweeps up and down, or up to eight of them, swept in turn. It is a [device](/docs/devices): a state, a panel, an engine and tools, each its own file.

## Controls

<DeviceControls device="chorus">
<Control id="rateHz">How fast the oscillator sweeps the delay.</Control>
<Control id="sync">Takes the rate from a note value and the tempo of the host. See [Tempo sync](/docs/devices#tempo-sync).</Control>
<Control id="division">The note value of one cycle of the oscillator.</Control>
<Control id="depth">How far the oscillator moves the delay.</Control>
<Control id="delayMs">The delay the sweep is centred on.</Control>
<Control id="voices">How many delays are read, 1 to 8, their oscillators spread evenly around the cycle. However many there are, they play as loud as one. A new count or a new delay with more than one voice waits for the knob to rest, then fades in.</Control>
<Control id="mix">The balance of the delayed signal and the dry one.</Control>
</DeviceControls>

## Panel

The panel draws the delay time of each voice over two seconds as its oscillator moves it. `chorusSweep(state, tempo, voice)` is what it draws for a voice. With Sync on, it draws the oscillator at the rate it really has.

## Engine

Create it, connect it and update it as for any device: see [The engine](/docs/devices#the-engine).

One voice is a delay node: the output of the oscillator, scaled by the depth, is added to its time. The sweep is kept inside the delay, so the time never goes negative.

Two to eight voices run on the audio thread: the kernel of `@audio/effect-chorus` in an `AudioWorklet` loaded from a blob (`CHORUS_WORKLET` is its source), 128 frames at a time, its depth the same sweep as a share of the delay. The kernel averages its voices, and on a full sound they hardly correlate, so the worklet raises them by the square root of their count. A worklet cannot import, so the device carries the kernel as published (`CHORUS_KERNELS`, in its `kernels.ts`). The worklet is fed only while more than one voice plays. Until it has loaded, and in a context with no worklets, the chorus plays one voice: `ready` resolves once it is in the graph.
