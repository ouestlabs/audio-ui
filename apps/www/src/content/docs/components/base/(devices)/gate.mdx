Cuts the sound between hits. Above the threshold the signal passes, and below it the signal is turned down to the floor. With the sidechain on, the clean model lets a kick open a held chord. The hysteresis model, from [audio.js](https://audio.js.org), closes only under a lower threshold than the one it opens at. It is a [device](/docs/devices): a state, a panel, an engine and tools, each its own file.

## Panel

The panel draws the transfer curve against the straight line of a clean sound, with a handle on the threshold. With the sidechain on, the curve is not drawn, because what the gate does depends on the other signal. The plot then keeps the threshold, which is a level of the sidechain.

## Engine

Create it, connect it and update it as for any device: see [The engine](/docs/devices#the-engine).

The browser has no gate node. For the clean model, the engine measures the level with an `AudioWorklet` that writes the gain to apply. The level is a peak follower. The gate is open while the level is over the threshold and for the hold after it, and the gain moves toward 1 or the floor with the attack or the release as a time constant.

A delay in front of the gain is the look-ahead. The detector reads the signal before it, so the gate is open when a transient arrives. `gateSettings(state)` gives the numbers the engine puts on its nodes.

Measured in Chrome, a hold of 100 ms keeps a quiet tone open for the 100 ms after a burst and then closes it to the floor, and 5 ms of look-ahead lets the first millisecond of a burst through at 0.50 instead of 0.26.

The hysteresis model is the processor of `@audio/dynamics-gate`'s manifest as published, in an `AudioWorklet` fed only while it plays, with its own look-ahead: the latency is the same for both models. Until the worklet is ready, the clean model plays for it. Every one of its values builds it again: at most every 50 ms, the new one first playing the last 100 ms of sound, then taking over in a 30 ms fade.
