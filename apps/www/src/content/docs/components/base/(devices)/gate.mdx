Cuts the sound between hits. Above the threshold the signal passes, and below it the signal is turned down to the floor. It is the gate of [audio.js](https://audio.js.org), which closes only under a lower threshold than the one it opens at. It is a [device](/docs/devices): a state, a panel, an engine and tools, each its own file.

## Controls

<DeviceControls device="gate">
<Control id="thresholdDb">The level above which the gate opens. Drag the handle on the plot across to set it.</Control>
<Control id="hysteresisDb">How far under the threshold the level must fall for the gate to close. The plot draws that level as a dotted line.</Control>
<Control id="floorDb">How far the signal is turned down while the gate is closed.</Control>
<Control id="attackMs">How fast the gate opens.</Control>
<Control id="holdMs">How long the gate stays open after the level drops under the threshold.</Control>
<Control id="releaseMs">How fast the gate closes after the hold.</Control>
<Control id="lookaheadMs">Delays the signal so the gate is open before a transient arrives. It adds the same [latency](/docs/devices#latency).</Control>
</DeviceControls>

## Panel

The panel draws the transfer curve against the straight line of a clean sound, with a handle on the threshold.

## Engine

Create it, connect it and update it as for any device: see [The engine](/docs/devices#the-engine).

The browser has no gate node. The engine is the processor of `@audio/dynamics-gate`'s manifest as published, in an `AudioWorklet`, with its own look-ahead. Until the worklet is ready the gate stays open. Every one of its values builds it again: at most every 50 ms, the new one first playing the last 100 ms of sound, then taking over in a 30 ms fade. `gateSettings(state)` gives the numbers the engine puts on it.
