Softens the sharp s and t of a voice. When the highs above a frequency get loud, they are taken down, never by more than the range. The split, band and wide band models, from [audio.js](https://audio.js.org), read how far they rise over the voice's body, so the same setting works at any recording level. It is a [device](/docs/devices): a state, a panel, an engine and tools, each its own file.

## Models

| Model | Tells an s by | Takes down |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Split | the highs rising over the voice's body | the highs over the frequency, by a linear-phase split |
| Band | the same | a band around the frequency, as wide as the Q |
| Wide band | the same | the whole sound, while the s lasts |

Split, Band and Wide band are the modes of `@audio/dynamics-deesser`, its processor as published in an `AudioWorklet`. They hear the sound 5 ms ahead of what they cut, so an s is taken down from its start: the device's [latency](/docs/devices#latency) is 5 ms. Until the worklet is ready, the highs pass unchanged. A change to one of their values builds the model again: at most every 50 ms, the new one first playing the last 100 ms of sound, then taking over in a 30 ms fade. Their knee, attack and release are the package's: 6 dB, 1 ms and 15 ms.

## Panel

The panel draws what the device does at each frequency at full reduction. The handle sits on the curve at the frequency: drag it across for the frequency and down for the range. `deesserResponse(state)` is the curve the panel draws. Pass a spectrum to the panel to see what comes out behind the curve.

## Engine

Create it, connect it and update it as for any device: see [The engine](/docs/devices#the-engine).

```ts
engine.reduction; // dB taken off the highs, read per frame
```

The panel draws the cut of the split with two Butterworth biquads in a row, a Linkwitz-Riley split, and the band with a band-pass of the same Q: at the frequency they match the package's linear-phase filters, which fall away faster around it. `engine.reduction` reads what is taken off the whole sound, which for a split or a band is less than what the highs lose.
