Softens the sharp s and t of a voice. When the highs above a frequency get loud, they are taken down, never by more than the range. The clean model reads their level against a threshold; the split, band and wide band models, from [audio.js](https://audio.js.org), read how far they rise over the voice's body, so the same setting works at any recording level. It is a [device](/docs/devices): a state, a panel, an engine and tools, each its own file.

## Models

| Model | Tells an s by | Takes down |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clean | the level of the highs over the threshold | the highs over the frequency, by a Linkwitz-Riley split |
| Split | the highs rising over the voice's body | the highs over the frequency, by a linear-phase split |
| Band | the same | a band around the frequency, as wide as the Q |
| Wide band | the same | the whole sound, while the s lasts |

Split, Band and Wide band are the modes of `@audio/dynamics-deesser`, its processor as published in an `AudioWorklet` fed only while one plays. They hear the sound 5 ms ahead of what they cut, so an s is taken down from its start: the device's [latency](/docs/devices#latency) is 5 ms with them, none with the clean model. Until the worklet is ready, the clean model plays for them. A change to one of their values builds the model again: at most every 50 ms, the new one first playing the last 100 ms of sound, then taking over in a 30 ms fade. Their knee, attack and release are the package's: 6 dB, 1 ms and 15 ms.

## Panel

The panel draws what the device does at each frequency at full reduction. The handle sits on the curve at the frequency: drag it across for the frequency and down for the range. `deesserResponse(state)` is the curve the panel draws. Pass a spectrum to the panel to see what comes out behind the curve.

## Engine

Create it, connect it and update it as for any device: see [The engine](/docs/devices#the-engine).

```ts
engine.reduction; // dB taken off the highs, read per frame
```

For the clean model, two biquads in a row make each half of a Linkwitz-Riley split, whose low and high halves add back to a flat response when nothing is taken down. A detector reads the highs and writes the gain they get. It is the one of the compressor's [sidechain](/docs/devices#sidechain), with a soft knee of 3 dB, an attack of 0.5 ms and a release of 60 ms. A table keeps the gain from going under the range.

Measured in Chrome, with nothing over the threshold the output is within 0.01 dB of the input from 200 Hz to 12 kHz. At full reduction a 10 kHz tone goes down by 5.1, 9.6 and 15.7 dB for ranges of 6, 12 and 24 dB, which is the curve the panel draws, to the hundredth.

For the split and band models, the panel draws the cut with the clean model's split and a band-pass of the same Q: at the frequency they match the package's linear-phase filters, which fall away faster around it. With the other models, `engine.reduction` reads what is taken off the whole sound, which for a split or a band is less than what the highs lose.
