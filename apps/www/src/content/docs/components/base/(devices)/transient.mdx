Shapes the attack and the sustain of a sound, at any level. A drum gains click or loses it, and a pluck rings longer or shorter. There is no threshold, so a quiet hit and a loud one are shaped alike. Its model is clean, or the follower of [audio.js](https://audio.js.org). It is a [device](/docs/devices): a state, a panel, an engine and tools, each its own file.

## Panel

The panel is a square with the sustain across and the attack up, and one handle to drag to both at once. The centre is a sound left as it is.

## Engine

Create it, connect it and update it as for any device: see [The engine](/docs/devices#the-engine).

The browser has no such node. For the clean model, a detector reads the signal as an `AudioWorklet` and writes the gain to apply. It follows the peak of the signal, which jumps up and falls over 15 ms so a low note does not ripple it, and the same peak smoothed over 25 ms. They meet when a sound holds and part when a hit starts. How far the peak is over the smoothed one says how much of the moment is attack (6 dB or more is all of it), and the rest is sustain. Only the shape of the envelope counts, not its level.

The audio waits a millisecond for the detector, so the gain is up when a hit arrives. That is the [latency](/docs/devices#latency) of the device.

Measured in Chrome on a decaying tone, the sustain moves the tail by what the control says, to the hundredth (+9 gives +9.00 dB and -9 gives -9.00 dB at 60, 200 and 2000 Hz). The attack leaves the tail alone, and +9 on the attack raises the first peak by 8.7 dB, at a level of -6 dB or of -26 dB alike. Taking the attack down lowers the peak by 6 to 7 dB for -9, since the gain needs a moment to fall.

The follower model is the processor of `@audio/dynamics-transient-shaper`'s manifest as published, in an `AudioWorklet` fed from the same delay, so the latency is the same for both models, and only while it plays: until it is ready, the clean model plays for it. Its attack and sustain change as it plays. On the same decaying tone, +9 on the attack raises the first peak by 8.9 dB at -6 dB and at -26 dB, -9 lowers it by 7.3 to 8.4 dB, and the sustain moves the tail by 8.7 to 9.0 dB, from 60 Hz to 2 kHz. A sustain turned up raises the first peak too, by about 1.5 dB: its gain blends with the attack's in a line.
