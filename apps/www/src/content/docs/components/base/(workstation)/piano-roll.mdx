A piano roll for writing a phrase: a keyboard down the side, notes on a grid, and a velocity bar under each note. It sits inside a [Timeline](/docs/components/base/arrangement), so notes use the timeline's units, snap to its grid and follow its zoom.

</ForFrameworks>

<ForFrameworks only="vue svelte web">

The piano roll's behavior is a few files, the primitive, written on the UI SDK kernel and copied into your project. It is a grid of MIDI notes you draw, move, resize and select, with rows by pitch (folded to the notes or to a scale), a marquee, bars for the velocity or the chance to play, and the commands of an editor on the keys. Each framework renders it with a thin adapter that you also own, and one suite of tests runs against each adapter.

</ForFrameworks>

## Overview

- **Notes:** press on the grid to draw a note or to start a selection rectangle, depending on the mode. Drag a note to move it, drag an edge to resize it, and double-click to delete it. Selected notes move and resize together.
- **Keys:** pressing a key on the side plays its note. The rows light up for the pitches playing.
- **Velocity:** each note has a bar in its own lane, drawn from the bottom.
- **Keyboard:** every edit works without a pointer, including copy, paste, duplicate and quantize.
- **Accessibility:** each note is a toggle button named after its pitch and span. Each velocity bar holds a native `<input type="range">`.

## Usage

<ForFrameworks only="react">

A note's `id` is a `string` or a `number`, so type the notes you keep with the roll's `Note`, or a state of literals such as `{ id: "a", … }` is narrower than what `onValueChange` gives back:

```tsx
import type { Note } from "@/components/audio-ui/piano-roll/piano-roll";

const [notes, setNotes] = useState<Note[]>([]);
```

```tsx
import { Timeline, TimelineNavigator } from "@/components/audio-ui/timeline";
import {
  PianoRoll,
  PianoRollGrid,
  PianoRollKeys,
  PianoRollStrip,
  PianoRollVelocity,
  PianoRollVelocityScale,
  PianoRollViewport,
} from "@/components/audio-ui/piano-roll";

<Timeline duration={16} snapStep={1}>
  <PianoRoll
    className="h-96"
    maxPitch={72}
    minPitch={48}
    onNoteOn={(pitch, details) => play(pitch, details.velocity)}
    onValueChange={setNotes}
    value={notes}
  >
    <PianoRollViewport>
      <PianoRollStrip className="flex-1" lead={<PianoRollKeys />}>
        <PianoRollGrid aria-label="Notes" gridStep={1} />
      </PianoRollStrip>
      <PianoRollStrip lead={<PianoRollVelocityScale />} sticky="bottom">
        <PianoRollVelocity gridStep={1} />
      </PianoRollStrip>
    </PianoRollViewport>
  </PianoRoll>
</Timeline>;
```

A note is `{ id, pitch, start, length, velocity?, deviation?, muted?, chance? }`: `pitch` is a MIDI number (60 is middle C), `start` and `length` are in the timeline's units, `velocity` is 0 to 1 (full when left out), `deviation` is how far the velocity may stray each time it plays (-1 to 1, up or down), `muted` deactivates it (it stays on the roll, silent) and `chance` is how likely it is to play each time round, 0 to 1. The roll draws them; playing them is yours, so a sequencer skips a muted note, rolls the dice for its chance and strays its velocity by up to its deviation. `value` and `selection` are controlled; `defaultValue` and `defaultSelection` leave them to the roll. The roll reports every edit through `onValueChange` and, when a gesture or a key press ends, `onValueCommitted`; `eventDetails.edit` tells what happened (`"add"`, `"move"`, `"resize"`, `"remove"`, `"duplicate"`, `"paste"`, `"quantize"`, `"stretch"`, `"velocity"`, `"chance"` or `"deviation"`) and `eventDetails.cancel()` keeps the notes as they were.

`onNoteOn` and `onNoteOff` say when a pitch should sound or stop: a key pressed, a note drawn, picked up or moved to a new pitch.

`scale` marks the rows of a scale (`data-in-scale` on each `PianoRoll.Row` in it, `data-out-of-scale` on the others, and `data-scale-root` on its root's): the styles leave the scale's rows clear, shade the others in place of the black keys, and edge the root's in the primary color, and `folded="scale"` keeps only those rows and the ones carrying notes. The roll takes a scale as its pitch classes and its root; `@/lib/engine/pitch` holds the scales by name (`SCALES`, `inScale`, `nearestInScale`) and `rollScale`, which gives one in that shape, and `edits.ts`, which installs with the roll, the edits a toolbar or a menu applies to the selection, each returning the new notes: `splitNotes`, `chopNotes`, `joinNotes`, `legatoNotes`, `reverseNotes`, `invertNotes`, `stretchNotes`, `fitNotesToScale`, `rampVelocity`, `humanizeNotes`, `toggleMuted`, `setChance`, `setDeviation` and `changeLevel`, and the time commands `deleteTime`, `insertTime` and `duplicateTime`; `selectionSpan` gives the time a selection covers and `findNotes` the notes a query matches, by pitch, velocity, length, chance, scale, state or count.

The toolbar of the preview above is three menus that install on their own: the [Scale menu](/docs/components/base/scale-menu) picks the scale the roll marks, [Note find](/docs/components/base/note-find) selects notes by what they are, and the [Note edit menu](/docs/components/base/note-edit-menu) runs the commands above on the selection.

### MusicXML files

The `musicxml-file` library does the same for a score, the format notation apps write: it reads a `.musicxml` or `.xml` file, or one compressed as `.mxl`, onto the roll's steps and writes one back. `npx shadcn@latest add @audio-ui/musicxml-file`.

```tsx
import {
  downloadMusicXmlFile,
  MUSICXML_FILE_ACCEPT,
  readMusicXmlFile,
} from "@/lib/engine/files";

const { bpm, notes, title } = await readMusicXmlFile(file, { duration: 64 });

<Button onClick={() => downloadMusicXmlFile(notes, { bpm, filename: "clip.musicxml" })}>
  Export as MusicXML
</Button>
```

`readMusicXmlFile` takes the grid `readMidiFile` takes and returns the score's first tempo and its title. It reads every part into the one clip, in the beats they were written in, through changes of tempo: chords, ties across the bar line, a second voice. It drops the grace notes, which take no time, plays the repeats once, as written, and reads a transposing instrument as written, not as it sounds. The score is parsed by `@audio/musicxml`. `writeMusicXmlFile` writes the notes on their grid, so a notation app shows them where they are. `readMusicXmlFileParts(file)` reads a score as parts, as `readMidiFileParts` does a MIDI file. A compressed score is opened with `fflate`.

### Driving it from an agent

`createPianoRollTools` gives a language model the tools to write on the roll from what a user asks ("a walking bassline in C minor", "up an octave"): `setNotes`, `addNotes`, `removeNotes`, `transpose`, `clear` and `setTempo`, each a description, a zod schema and an `apply` that returns the next state. `npx shadcn@latest add @audio-ui/piano-roll-tools`.

```tsx
import { tool } from "ai";
import { createPianoRollTools } from "@/lib/piano-roll-tools";

const roll = createPianoRollTools({ maxPitch: 72, minPitch: 48, stepCount: 16 });

const tools = Object.fromEntries(
  Object.entries(roll).map(([name, t]) => [
    name,
    tool({
      description: t.description,
      execute: (args) => {
        setState((state) => t.apply(state, args as never));
        return `applied ${name}`;
      },
      inputSchema: t.schema,
    }),
  ])
);
```

The pitch range and the grid are in the schemas, and every note a model writes is clamped into them, so whatever it answers lands on the roll's rows and steps. The tools read and write `{ notes, bpm }` and depend on no agent library: the example wires them to the AI SDK, and any tool-calling API takes the same description and schema.

A Root holds the notes and their selection. The roll is a column of parts you place in it:

- **Grid**: the surface the notes are drawn on, a lane of the timeline. A press draws a note in draw mode, or starts a marquee in select mode; a double click adds or removes a note; Enter adds one at the playback position.
- **Rows** and **Row**: a row for each pitch drawn, to style from `data-color`, `data-in-scale`, `data-scale-root`, `data-octave` and `data-active`.
- **Notes** and **Note**: a note for each note of the roll, in reading order. A press selects it and a drag moves it. A **NoteHandle** resizes an edge.
- **Marquee**: the box a drag across the bare grid draws. **SelectionSpan** shows the stretch the selection covers, and a **StretchHandle** in it stretches the selected notes.
- **VelocityLane** and **Velocity**: a bar under each note, whose height is its velocity (or its chance, with `level="chance"`), set by a drag or through its native input.
- **Keys**: gives the pitches drawn, the ones sounding and the audition calls, to draw a vertical [piano](/docs/components/base/piano#installation) beside the grid. The part is a grid of one cell, so the piano drawn inside takes its whole height and width: stretch the part beside the grid, and the keys line up with the rows.

### The timeline it sits on

The roll reads a timeline's length, grid, zoom and playback position. The UI SDK has no provider to read them from, so you give them to the Root, as the values you give the [Timeline](/docs/components/base/arrangement#installation):

- `duration`, `snapStep`, `snapOffset` and `snapPoints`: the length, the grid every gesture lands on unless Cmd/Ctrl is held, and what pulls a note onto it.
- `zoom`: px per unit, or unset while the roll fits its width. Control it, and give the same value to the Timeline, so the roll and the ruler stay aligned.
- `source` or `position`: the playback position, which Enter and the shortcuts that add at the playhead read. A `source` is a live value in the shape of an external store.

The Grid and the VelocityLane are lanes the Timeline's viewport finds in its DOM, so a roll inside a Timeline's Viewport scrolls and zooms with the ruler.

### Pointer

<KeyRow gesture={"Drag a velocity bar"}>Changes the velocity of the selection by the same amount.</KeyRow>
<KeyRow gesture={"`Cmd` / `Ctrl` + drag a velocity bar"}>Sets how far the velocity of the selection may stray each time it plays.</KeyRow>
<KeyRow gesture={"Drag across a velocity or chance lane"}>With `mode="draw"`, paints every bar under the pointer to its height.</KeyRow>

Set on the Root, as props (an attribute in kebab case in the web adapter, the arrays as JSON).

A Note, a NoteHandle and a Velocity take the `note` they belong to. A Velocity takes `level` (`"velocity"` or `"chance"`), and so does the VelocityLane.

### What a change reports

`details` carries:

- `reason`: what changed the notes, such as `"draw"`, `"drag"`, `"keyboard"`, `"paste"` or a command's name.
- `source`: `"mouse"`, `"touch"`, `"pen"`, `"keyboard"`, `"programmatic"` or `"agent"`.
- `event` and `cancel()`: the DOM event behind it, and the refusal. A controlled roll keeps the notes you give it.

In the web adapter, the notes are the `input` and `change` events, and the selection and the keys are `selectionchange`, `noteon` and `noteoff`, with a `detail` of the value and its reason. `event.preventDefault()` refuses a change.

### States to style from

- On the Root and every part: `data-mode`, `data-editing` while a pointer edits, `data-folded`, `data-disabled`; on the Root `data-has-selection`.
- On a note and its parts: `data-selected`, `data-muted`, `data-note` for its id. A note sets `--piano-roll-velocity` and `--piano-roll-chance`, a bar `--piano-roll-level` and `--piano-roll-deviation`, for what you draw inside them.
- On a row: `data-color` (`black` or `white`), `data-in-scale`, `data-out-of-scale`, `data-scale-root`, `data-octave`, `data-active`, and `data-pitch`.
