## Usage

`WaveformLane` draws the waveforms of all of a lane's clips on one canvas, each in its own part of it, placed as the clips are on the lane. Drawn by `gl-waveform` on the GPU, every pixel column shows the exact lowest and highest of its samples with the RMS inside, and zoomed in it is a line through the samples, with a dot on each: at any zoom, over recordings of any length. It is a thin React wrapper over [`@audio-ui/gl/waveform`](/docs/gl/waveform), which does the drawing and has no React in it: a custom element or a script starts the same lane. All the clips share one WebGL context, and clips of one recording share what is on the GPU for it. The audio editor switches its lane between this, the spectrogram and the waveform by band from its View menu.

```tsx
import { WaveformLane } from "@/components/audio-ui/waveform-lane";

<WaveformLane
  className="h-full text-foreground"
  clips={clips} // { id, source, start, length, gain, peak, sampleRate, samples, segments, bands? }[]
  look="plain" // or "bands"
  zoom={pixelsPerSecond}
/>;
```

| Prop | Type | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `clips` | `WaveformClip[]` | The clips to draw: `id`, the `source` recording they read, where each `start`s and how `length` it is on the lane in seconds, its `gain`, the recording's `peak` (its loudest sample, 0 to 1, drawn as tall as the lane), `sampleRate` and `samples` (`{ frames, read }`, mixed to one), `segments` (how it reads its recording, see below) and `bands` for the band look. |
| `zoom` | `number` | The lane's pixels a second. |
| `look` | `"plain" \| "bands"` | One waveform, or the three bands of the sound one over the other. Default `"plain"`. |

With `look="bands"` a clip is drawn as its sound split in three by a Linkwitz-Riley crossover, at 250 Hz and 2.5 kHz (`splitBands` of [`@audio-ui/engine/analysis`](/docs/engine/analysis)): the lows warm, the mids in the ink's own colour and the highs cool, the lows tall behind the highs, as DJ software colours a sound. Each band is drawn as tall as its own loudest sample reaches (`peaks`), so a band that is quiet in the mix still shows. `bands` is called once a recording and may split it lazily: the split holds three copies of the mix.

A clip says how it reads its recording as `segments(from, to)`: the stretches of the clip between two times of it, each a straight read, as a DAW paints a region. [`clipSegments`](/docs/lib/arrangement) of the arrangement library gives them for a clip that plays on from its offset, one for each turn of its loop and each stretch between its warp markers. A stretch read backwards is not drawn, so a reversed clip is [`ClipWaveform`](/docs/components/base/arrangement)'s, which draws a clip as it plays, as it is where `hasWebGL2` of `@audio-ui/gl/lane` says WebGL 2 is missing. The color is the text color.
