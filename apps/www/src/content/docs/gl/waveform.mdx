## Usage

`createWaveformLane` draws the waveforms of a lane's clips on a canvas in the lane's box, each clip in its own parts of it. Drawn by `gl-waveform`, every pixel column shows the exact lowest and highest of its samples with the RMS inside, and zoomed in it is a line through the samples with a dot on each: at any zoom, over recordings of any length. The color is the canvas' text color.

```ts
import { createWaveformLane } from "@audio-ui/gl/waveform";

const lane = createWaveformLane(canvas, {
  clips, // WaveformClip[]: a LaneClip with the recording's `peak`, and `bands` for the band look
  layout: "auto", // a row a channel once the rows are tall enough; or "split", "mix"
  look: "plain", // or "bands"
  zoom: pixelsPerSecond,
});

lane.update({ clips, look: "bands", zoom });
lane.destroy();
```

A clip's `peak` is its recording's loudest sample, 0 to 1: the waveform is drawn as tall as the loudest reaches the lane's height, as a quiet recording still fills its lane. The clip's `gain` scales what is drawn, as it scales what plays.

## Channels

A recording with several channels is drawn as a DAW draws it: a row for each channel once the rows are tall enough to read, one waveform below that, as a short track shows one lane. `layout` is `"auto"` by default and splits when each row would be at least `SPLIT_ROW_PX` (40) CSS px tall; `"split"` and `"mix"` force it. Give a clip its `channels`, one reader each, for the rows; `samples` stays their mix, which the one waveform, the band look and the spectrogram draw. The rows share the clip's `peak`, so channels are compared at the same height, and are told apart by a pixel of ground. `splitsChannels` and `waveformRows` give the layout for a lane height, to lay out what goes beside it.

## By band

With `look: "bands"` a clip is drawn as the three bands of its sound one over the other: the lows warm behind, the mids in the ink's colour, the highs cool in front, as DJ software colours a sound. A clip's `bands()` gives the sound split in three (`splitBands` of [`@audio-ui/engine/analysis`](/docs/engine/analysis)), with the loudest sample of each band so each is drawn as tall as its own loudest reaches. It is called once a recording and may split lazily: the split holds three copies of the mix.

## Reading backwards

`gl-waveform` has no range that reads backwards: a stretch whose frames fall is not drawn. A reversed clip is the host's own drawing, and a page draws it that way too where WebGL 2 is missing.
