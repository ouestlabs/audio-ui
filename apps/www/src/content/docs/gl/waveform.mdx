## Usage

`createWaveformLane` draws the waveforms of a lane's clips on a canvas in the lane's box, each clip in its own parts of it. Drawn by `gl-waveform`, every pixel column shows the exact lowest and highest of its samples with the RMS inside, and zoomed in it is a line through the samples with a dot on each: at any zoom, over recordings of any length. The color is the canvas' text color.

```ts
import { createWaveformLane } from "@audio-ui/gl/waveform";

const lane = createWaveformLane(canvas, {
  clips, // WaveformClip[]: a LaneClip with the recording's `peak`, and `bands` for the band look
  layout: "auto", // a row a channel once the rows are tall enough; or "split", "mix"
  look: "plain", // or "bands"
  zoom: pixelsPerSecond,
});

lane.update({ clips, look: "bands", zoom });
lane.destroy();
```

A clip's `peak` is its recording's loudest sample, 0 to 1: the waveform is drawn as tall as the loudest reaches the lane's height, as a quiet recording still fills its lane. The clip's `gain` scales what is drawn, as it scales what plays.

## Many lanes

A browser keeps some sixteen WebGL contexts and loses the oldest past that. `createWaveformLane(canvas, inputs, { shared: true })` draws on the page's one context instead, a canvas never on the page, and copies the picture onto `canvas`, a 2D one: a page draws as many lanes as it has clips, one a clip as an arrangement draws them, and lanes reading one recording share its renderers, each let go of once no lane draws it. Leave `zoom` out for a lane that fits its clips to its box, and a clip's `peak` for the lane to read it, from its leaves or its samples. A lane of its own lets go of its context when it is destroyed (`releaseContext` of `@audio-ui/gl/lane`), unless it starts again on the same canvas at once, as React does in development.

## Streamed recordings

A recording streamed from a file is never read whole. Its `samples` give `leaves`: the recording summed up 256 frames at a time, `[min, max, Σx², count]` each, as `waveformLeaves` makes them from its samples, once, as it is written, and kept beside it. Zoomed out the lane draws from them alone, exact at a leaf a device pixel or more; past that it reads the frames it shows, a chunk of `CHUNK_FRAMES` (65536) at a time, and lets go of the oldest chunks it no longer shows. `leavesPeak` gives the clip's `peak` from them.

```ts
import { leavesPeak, waveformLeaves } from "@audio-ui/gl/waveform";

// Once, while the recording is written: whole leaves a stretch at a time, laid end to end.
const leaves = waveformLeaves(mix);

const samples = {
  frames,
  leaves: () => leaves, // or a promise, read from where they are kept
  read: (from, to) => readFrames(from, to), // a chunk at a time, at once or as a promise
};
const peak = leavesPeak(leaves);
```

Each channel of a clip's `channels` gives its own leaves for its row.

## Channels

A recording with several channels is drawn as a DAW draws it: a row for each channel once the rows are tall enough to read, one waveform below that, as a short track shows one lane. `layout` is `"auto"` by default and splits when each row would be at least `SPLIT_ROW_PX` (40) CSS px tall; `"split"` and `"mix"` force it. Give a clip its `channels`, one reader each, for the rows; `samples` stays their mix, which the one waveform, the band look and the spectrogram draw. The rows share the clip's `peak`, so channels are compared at the same height, and are told apart by a pixel of ground. `splitsChannels` and `waveformRows` give the layout for a lane height, to lay out what goes beside it.

## By band

With `look: "bands"` a clip is drawn as the three bands of its sound one over the other: the lows warm behind, the mids in the ink's colour, the highs cool in front, as DJ software colours a sound. A clip's `bands()` gives the sound split in three (`splitBands` of [`@audio-ui/engine/analysis`](/docs/engine/analysis)), with the loudest sample of each band so each is drawn as tall as its own loudest reaches. It is called once a recording and may split lazily: the split holds three copies of the mix. A recording streamed from a file gives each band as a reader instead (`{ frames, read, leaves }`), whose leaves are made with the others in the same pass (`createBandSplit` of `@audio-ui/engine/analysis`) and whose stretches are split as the view reads them; a band given no peak is drawn as tall as its leaves' loudest.

## Reading backwards

`gl-waveform` has no range that reads backwards: a stretch whose frames fall is not drawn. A reversed clip is the host's own drawing, and a page draws it that way too where WebGL 2 is missing.
